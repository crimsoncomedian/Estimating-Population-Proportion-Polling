Election Polling: Estimate Voter Support

An election is approaching. What percentage of all eligible voters would vote for Candidate A if the election were held today? You cannot survey everyone, so conduct one random poll and build a confidence interval.

1. Unknown population proportion

The percentage of all eligible voters who support Candidate A is unknown, represented by p. The true proportion stays hidden until you finish the poll. Both graphs use the same 0–100% support scale.

2. Conduct a poll and construct a confidence interval

Complete one step at a time. The instruction area changes after each answer, while the poll graph remains visible throughout.

Calculate the estimated standard error

Use your sample proportion p̂ as a stand-in for the unknown population proportion p.

SE=p^(1−p^)n

Choose a confidence level and find the critical value

Find the lower and upper endpoints

Subtract and add the margin of error to p̂. Enter answers as percentages, not decimals. The confidence interval appears on the graph after both endpoints are correct.

Lower = p̂ − margin of error   |   Upper = p̂ + margin of error

Using this one poll, you've estimated a plausible range for the percentage of all eligible voters who support Candidate A. The confidence level describes the long-run success rate of this method under its assumptions; it does not guarantee this particular poll's interval contains the true percentage.

Model: independent random responses from an unchanged population, with each voter supporting Candidate A or not. This activity uses the usual one-proportion z-interval. Step 3 uses a standard normal (z) critical value, not a t value. The success–failure condition requires at least 10 sampled supporters and 10 other voters. Enter SE and margin of error as decimals (approximately three decimal places); endpoints as percentages (two decimal places).
